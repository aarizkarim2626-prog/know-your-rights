On gender equality & the law that protects you at work

Most people who face discrimination at work never learn that what happened to them has a section number attached to it — a legal definition, a punishment, and an office required to act on it.

The Act, Article by Article

Harassment isn't limited to sexual conduct. Since the 2022 Amendment, it also includes discrimination based on gender alone — any conduct or attitude that interferes with your work or creates a hostile, intimidating, or offensive environment, even without a sexual element.

It is also a criminal offence in its own right under Section 509 of the Pakistan Penal Code, separate from any workplace committee process.

PAHWWA 2010, §2(h)(ii) — 2022 Amendment · PPC §509

The 2022 Amendment widened "employee" well beyond the office worker. It now covers freelancers, gig workers, domestic workers, interns, trainees, and apprentices — paid or unpaid — plus students, performers, and former employees who resigned or were dismissed.

The law also uses gender-neutral language, so men and transgender individuals are protected too — men filed roughly 40% of recent FOSPAH complaints.

PAHWWA 2010, as amended 2022
Verified provision

"Workplace" no longer means just an office. It now reaches educational institutions, gig and delivery platforms, remote work, work travel, off-site events, and any setting connected to your job.

PAHWWA 2010, "workplace" redefinition
Verified provision

Every organization in Pakistan is legally required to have an Inquiry Committee — a standing obligation, not a favor. If none exists, or you don't trust it, you can go straight to FOSPAH (the Federal Ombudsperson) or your provincial ombudsperson.

You are not required to exhaust the internal process first, and you can pursue a criminal complaint with police under PPC §509 at the same time as a FOSPAH case.

If This Is Happening to You
Four steps, in order. Each one strengthens the ones after it.

Document everything

Dates, times, what was said or done, and who else was present. Save messages as you receive them.

Report to your Inquiry Committee

Every workplace is legally required to have one. Submit your complaint in writing and keep a copy.

Escalate to FOSPAH if unresolved

No committee, or no action? The Federal or provincial Ombudsperson can take your complaint directly.

Reach out for support

Legal aid organizations and NGOs can guide you through the process at no cost.

Case Summary

The problem

Legal protection against workplace gender discrimination in Pakistan is strong on paper, but awareness is not. Most affected workers don't know the specific law, section, or office that applies to their situation — so violations go unreported.

The solution

This guide translates the amended Protection Against Harassment Act into plain, navigable language, opening with a quiz that corrects common myths people are told about what the law actually allows.

The expected impact

By closing the awareness gap, this tool helps people recognize discrimination early, push back on false claims about "how it works," and use the legal channels already built for them.
